What it is

Plain about what it does. Plainer about what it doesn't.

TorVPN (the app calls itself "Tor VPN Client") bundles the Tor software and routes traffic into it through Android's VPN service. This page describes the v0.1 test build, read directly from its release file.

Features

Built around what you control.

What the released v0.1 Android build contains, read from its manifest and code. It has two screens: controls, and diagnostics. No country or server picker, and no settings screen.

IN THE v0.1 BUILD

Two switches, in order

Tor and the VPN start separately. Tap Start Tor, wait for it to be ready, then tap Start VPN. Each has its own Stop button.

IN THE v0.1 BUILD

Tor through the VPN interface

Android's VPN service opens a tunnel (tun0, with IPv4 and IPv6 addresses) and a local bridge hands its traffic to the Tor client bundled in the app. Only TCP is carried; UDP and ICMP are switched off.

IN THE v0.1 BUILD

Identity rotation, automatic

While connected, the app asks Tor for a new identity every five minutes, then checks the exit address and notes whether it changed. The interval is fixed in this build.

IN THE v0.1 BUILD

Clear status and failures

The app tracks Tor as stopped, starting, bootstrapping, ready or failed, and the VPN as disconnected, starting, connected, stopping or error. If Tor hasn't answered within 60 seconds, it stops waiting and says so.

IN THE v0.1 BUILD

A diagnostics screen

Shows the current startup stage and Tor state, plus the Android version, device and app version. Run Tor on its own as a test, then copy, export or clear the log.

How it works

Only the known parts, drawn.

Read from the v0.1 build: apps send TCP traffic into Android's VPN interface, a local bridge hands it to the bundled Tor client, and Tor carries it onward. The app starts Tor first, then the VPN. What the destination sees depends on Tor and the site.

Privacy & security

Evidence before claims.

No audits, badges or guarantees appear here unless they exist. TorVPN is not presented as affiliated with the Tor Project.

Found in the v0.1 build

  • Runs as an Android VPN service with a bundled Tor client.
  • Requests only internet and foreground-service permissions.
  • Carries TCP only: UDP and ICMP are switched off.
  • Checks its exit address with check.torproject.org, through its local Tor connection.
  • Keeps a diagnostics log you can copy, export or clear.

Documented by the owner

  • No privacy policy or data statement published yet.

Not yet established

  • Whether logs ever leave the device.
  • How DNS lookups behave in practice; the tunnel config maps DNS, untested here.
  • Behavior if the connection drops.
  • Any independent audit.
Getting started

Three steps, once confirmed.

  1. Download the APK

    v0.1 is a 41 MB debug build for Android, published on GitHub, not in an app store. It is an early test release; the app reports version 0.6.0-vpn-tor-bridge.

    View the v0.1 release ↗

  2. Install

    Needs Android 10 or newer. Installing an APK from outside a store means allowing your browser or file manager to install apps.

  3. Connect

    Android asks you to approve a VPN connection. In the app, tap Start Tor and wait until it is ready, then tap Start VPN. Android asks you to approve the VPN connection.
